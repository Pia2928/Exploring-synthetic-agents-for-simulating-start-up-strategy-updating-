Presenting stimuli — research guidance

    
    
      
  1. Write stimuli the way real feedback actually sounds. A customer, investor, or co-founder rarely gives clean, unambiguous feedback. Mixed, hedged, or contradictory signals are more diagnostic than a flat "the customer said no."
    2. 
      
  2. Vary one thing at a time when comparing agents. If you want to isolate the effect of alignment strategy, hold founder identity, demographics, and temperature constant across the agents you're comparing, and change only the strategy.
    3. 
      
  3. Run Individual before Group when possible. Individual reactions establish each agent's unmediated baseline, so you can later see how much a stance shifted due to social context rather than the agent's own reasoning.
    4. 
      
  4. Do not tell the agent it is being tested or simulated. An agent that knows it's being benchmarked tends to produce more careful, less naturalistic answers — the universal instructions already avoid this, keep your stimuli consistent with it.
    5. 
      
  5. Use repeated runs for anything you plan to generalise from. A single reaction from a single agent is one data point, not a pattern. Rerunning the same stimulus (particularly with varied temperature) shows the spread of plausible reactions.
    6. 
      
  6. Keep some stimuli deliberately ambiguous. A stimulus with one obvious correct reading tells you little about how an agent actually reasons under uncertainty.
